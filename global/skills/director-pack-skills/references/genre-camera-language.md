# Genre, medium and viewpoint camera language

Use with [camera techniques](camera-techniques.md). These are selectable production starting points, not mandatory genre laws or MiniMax guarantees. “Cinematic” is a quality/style request, “live action” and “anime” are media, “found footage” is a presentation/viewpoint, and “noir” is a stylistic/narrative tradition. Combine them deliberately rather than treating the labels as interchangeable. The current user wants plausible cameras in every combination.

This table covers camera and staging only. For a genre's full scene grammar (coverage, cutting rhythm, performance, sound and medium adjustments) and for writing a project's direction profile, use [genre direction profiles](genre-direction-profiles.md).

Choose a dominant visual approach, then name the deviation at the shot that needs it. Project locks and the actual scene take precedence. Never paste every row into a generation prompt. Practitioner evidence and the distinction between research and these recommendations are in [research sources](cinematic-research-sources.md).

| Mode | Useful camera/staging approach | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Cinematic live-action drama | Grounded eye height, readable blocking, purposeful static compositions; measured push, pull or lateral reveal when attention changes. Let performance carry the shot. | Constant small pushes on every face, decorative orbiting, ultra-shallow focus hiding the action. |
| Found footage / recovered recording | Define who records, the device and how it is carried or placed. Use operator-height framing, limited reaction delay, partial occlusion and believable reframing. A camera set on a table stays there until retrieved. | Omniscient reverse angles, unexplained overhead shots, a dropped camera floating back up, routine violent shake. |
| Documentary / observational | A physically present observer finds or follows events. Controlled shoulder work, tripod observation, practical reframing, a motivated zoom from a fixed distant position. Preserve the subject's autonomy within frame. | Predicting every surprise, floating through crowds, polishing every moment into an advertisement, excessive fake focus errors. |
| Interview / explanatory documentary | Stable eyeline near interviewer, deliberate headroom, restrained push only if chosen; relevant inserts and context shots connected in the edit. | Mouth animation driven by narration, unmotivated camera circles, invented interview answers. |
| Wildlife / nature | Plausible distant observation, long-lens supported following, static behavior coverage; aerials only as separately established viewpoints. | Instant macro-to-mountain flight, invasive camera travel through an animal, impossible focus/depth combinations. |
| Anime / drawn 2D | Designed silhouettes, readable screen direction, purposeful static layouts, lateral pan/tracking or multiplane depth when authored. Anticipation, travel, contact and follow-through occur in the drawing, not solely in camera motion. | Treating a still panel as a whole action; default 3D orbit that remodels faces; importing glossy live-action optics or exact exposure claims. |
| Sports / battle anime (TV) | Two registers. Everyday scenes: grounded eye-level staging and shot / reverse-shot dialogue. Matches: dynamic diagonal layouts, low angles on competitors, fast cutting between player, object and opponent, held impact frames, speed lines and smears for velocity. | Battle grammar in quiet scenes; static medium shots through a match; slow push-ins in place of cuts; unauthorized glow effects. |
| 2.5D / cel-shaded 3D | Choose whether depth comes from layered artwork or modeled space. Keep layers spatially coherent; camera paths expose only designed geometry. | Cardboard stretching, texture swimming, inventing unseen sides during a large orbit. |
| Stop-motion / miniature | Deliberate framing, stable scale, supported incremental camera travel and authored pose cadence; physical weight survives the style. | Universal micro-jitter, inadvertent scale changes, smoothing that conflicts with the chosen finish. |
| Noir / neo-noir | Deep staged relationships, partial concealment, motivated hard pools of light and shadow. Static frames, deliberate lateral reveals or restrained approaches can expose power and uncertainty. Color noir is valid. | Automatic Dutch tilt, rain/smoke in every scene, black-and-white as the whole definition, turning every reveal into a fast orbit. |
| Horror / suspense | Define what is withheld. Hold negative space, track toward an uncertain reveal or let an offscreen sound motivate a pivot; preserve geography so intrusion is legible. | A roaming camera that reveals the threat before intended, constant shake, impossible corridors or unrequested monster effects. |
| Thriller / crime / surveillance | Sightlines, concealment, foreground cover and restricted viewpoints; supported long-lens observation or purposeful pursuit. | Camera knowing hidden information without a viewpoint change, random cants replacing tension. |
| Action / martial arts | Establish attack direction and space; side or three-quarter coverage makes contact readable. Follow the actor or use a wide composition, then cut for consequence. | Hiding every impact behind shake/blur, turning normal action into slow motion, moves that cross through combatants. |
| War / disaster | Match perspective to an observer, participant or clearly established objective camera. Controlled pursuit, cover-based viewpoints and specific disturbances. | Perpetual tremor, viewpoint flying from shoulder to aerial, explosions used to conceal continuity defects. |
| Sports / chase | Sideline follow pan, parallel tracking vehicle or established overhead. Keep speed legible through ground travel, strides and background parallax. Replays are separate authored timing treatments. | Unintentional speed ramps, tracking faster than an implausible platform, direction reversals across cuts. |
| Romance / intimacy | Shared frames, eye contact and spatial distance; a gentle motivated approach or sideways reveal can change intimacy. | Mandatory orbiting kiss, automatic facial push-in, camera passing between touching bodies. |
| Comedy | Frame setup and payoff together when possible; use a held wide for blocking or a timed reframe to reveal the joke. | Moving early and spoiling the reveal, routine crash zooms, cutting away before the physical payoff. |
| Period / western / epic | Deliberate geography, group staging, measured lateral travel and justified crane/landscape reveals. Use scale without losing faces or direction. | Every exterior becoming a drone shot, endless aerial pullbacks, modern camera artifacts added without a stylistic decision. |
| Science fiction / fantasy | Treat the camera as physical even when the subject/world is extraordinary. Establish rules for vehicles, gravity and mechanisms; cut for impossible changes of scale. | Using fictional technology as automatic permission for camera teleportation or geometry morphing. |
| Musical / dance / music video | Choreograph performers and camera together. A supported track/arc can preserve full-body movement; cuts follow chosen accents without interrupting every gesture. | Camera motion that hides footwork, automatic beat-sync without source/timing, repeated speed ramps. |
| Product / fashion / architectural | Precise short slider, turntable-versus-camera distinction, bounded orbit or static texture/detail shot; preserve object geometry and lighting. | Rotating both object and camera ambiguously, fly-through solid surfaces, automatic melting transitions. |

## Adaptable shot examples

These fragments are original direction examples. Insert them into the selected H3 format only after resolving the actual cast, setting, references and duration. Do not copy the invented scene into another movie.

**Found footage:** A handheld consumer camera remains at the recorder's chest height near the back door. When a scrape sounds on the porch, the recorder turns right toward the sound, briefly overshoots the doorframe and corrects once. The camera stays inside the room; the doorway blocks the unseen part of the porch. No cut occurs.

**Observational documentary:** From beside the workbench, a shoulder-held medium-wide shot follows the mechanic's hands as she lifts the repaired wheel. The operator takes one small lateral step to keep the contact point visible and settles. Her working pace stays natural. Workshop light and room sound remain consistent.

**Anime action:** A drawn side-view composition holds the runner and the ledge in the same layout. The front foot plants, the body drives forward and the runner clears the gap before landing into bent knees. A short lateral track maintains the screen direction while foreground posts pass; the camera does not circle into an unestablished view. Pose spacing conveys fast travel.

**Noir:** A static medium-wide frame separates the seated investigator from the visitor behind the frosted doorway. A hard corridor light outlines the visitor; the desk lamp lights the investigator's hands. As the door opens, a short lateral dolly reveals the visitor's face from behind the jamb, then settles. The lighting sources stay fixed in the room.

**Live-action drama:** Eye-level two-shot beside a window. As one character places the key between them and withdraws the hand, the camera advances a short distance along the clear side of the table, ending where the key and both reactions remain readable. The move does not stretch the placement into slow motion.

**Action:** A three-quarter wide shot shows the defender step aside, the attacker pass the former position, and both regain footing. The camera pans from its fixed position to retain them; it does not swing through either body. Cut to the consequence only after the decisive interaction is readable.

## Consistency across a sequence

Keep a short project camera baseline: viewpoint, support style, usual framing/depth, movement energy, cut rhythm and deliberate exceptions. Re-state only what the current segment needs. For found footage, track the recorder and device even when offscreen. For anime, track layout perspective and layer depth as carefully as identity. Preserve lighting direction, action axis and camera side at joins. A scene may intensify or calm down; express that progression through authored changes instead of randomly sampling techniques.
