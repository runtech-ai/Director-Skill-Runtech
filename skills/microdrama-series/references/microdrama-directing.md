# Microdrama directing

This is the creative directing method for microdrama. The Director Pack remains the serialization and importer contract. Its legacy creative procedure is not a prerequisite. Use the current brief, source and this method to create one authoritative plan before rendering boards or compiling H3 prompts.

## Dramatic scene and coverage

For each scene identify the immediate want, obstacle, tactic, turning event and changed relationship or knowledge. Stage observable decisions: a character conceals a receipt, withholds a hand, blocks an exit or chooses to answer. Avoid prescribing a facial tic on every word. Plan who perceives each reveal and when the audience learns it. Preserve locked dialogue and cultural context.

Choose coverage around these changes. Establish only the geography needed for the next action; a two-shot can establish conflict while the scene is already underway. Use a closer view when new information or a change of power needs it. An insert must reveal something the audience must read. Cut to a listener when their response changes the meaning, rather than alternating faces mechanically. End the episode on a consequential revelation, decision or threat; keep a physically moving object moving through that ending. Suspense does not require a frozen projectile.

Portrait delivery needs deliberate depth, vertical blocking and readable hands/props. Compose the actual delivery frame separately from the board-sheet canvas. Check intended UI-safe regions for the target platform instead of assuming universal pixel margins. Do not crop away the action that explains the turn.

## Timing and performance

Budget speech, action and reaction separately on the same timeline. Short episodes do not require every shot to be short, equal duration, or densely moving. Time exact dialogue at natural delivery and allow intelligible final words. A silent listener may walk, breathe, manipulate an object or change expression; silence concerns speech, not whole-body stillness. Mouth direction must remain compatible with speaking.

For each significant physical event state the initial support/contact, preparation, release or transfer, travel, contact and follow-through. Make the event fit its plausible timescale, not the whole shot. If a thrown object crosses the frame quickly, spend remaining screen time on the consequence, recovery or another motivated beat. Do not stretch travel across six seconds merely because the clip lasts six seconds. Preserve gravity, momentum, collisions, grip changes and object ownership unless the story explicitly establishes a stylized mechanism. Animation style alone does not imply floaty motion.

Fast pacing comes from meaningful change, economical coverage and cuts. It does not mean accelerated speech, a camera rushing everywhere, or constant close-ups. Slow motion is a deliberate local choice with a defined start/end, not a default supplied by words such as cinematic or epic. Frame rate and playback retiming are separate from action direction.

## Camera with a credible operator

Choose the viewpoint before choosing movement. Identify support, starting position, ending position, clear path, subject distance and the event motivating the move. A tripod, shoulder camera, dolly, stabilizer, crane, vehicle mount or aerial platform has different access and inertia. Use a locked frame when the performance carries the beat. Coordinate pan/tilt with a tracking move when needed; avoid contradictory lens and movement requests.

Do not move through walls, people, closed windows or furniture; teleport to another viewpoint; orbit through inaccessible space; zoom through an eye; or instantly accelerate a heavy rig. Use a cut when the desired destination has no credible path. A zoom changes framing without reproducing a dolly's perspective change. Focus transitions require subjects at different focus distances; they are not camera travel. Keep screen direction and eyelines coherent, and visibly motivate an axis crossing.

Style choices are project choices:
- Live-action drama/romance: proximity, restrained reframing, motivated movement toward an emotional change; stillness is valid.
- Thriller/horror: controlled reveals, obscured sightlines, credible searching viewpoints and withheld information; no default floating camera.
- Action: readable preparation, trajectory, contact and consequence; establish geography before tighter coverage.
- Comedy: preserve the setup/payoff relationship, performance timing and necessary shared frame.
- Found footage: identify who holds the device, why they record, what they can see, and how movement affects their body. Shake follows actual steps or impacts, not a constant preset.
- Documentary: a plausible observer reacts to unscripted behavior, adjusts framing and focus, and cannot anticipate unseen events perfectly.
- Noir: use motivated shadow, depth, concealment and power relationships; noir does not require an orbit or Dutch angle in every shot.
- Anime: use deliberate layouts, readable silhouettes and consistent spatial mechanics. Stylized impact frames or speed lines do not authorize impossible camera paths or indefinite airborne poses.

For a larger menu, consult the repaired camera-techniques and genre-camera-language references through the contract boundary guide. They are optional craft resources; the microdrama plan remains the creative authority.

## Storyboard roles and H3 handoff

Author the shots first. Make exactly the existing contract's panel count and layout; never manufacture shots to fill the sheet. In accompanying direction prose identify each panel as entry, intermediate action or exit evidence. Specify the pictured instant and the motion before/after it. A spear shown mid-flight is a sampled instant, not a requirement to keep that position. Boards establish identity, composition, geography and ordered beats; text establishes temporal evolution. Preserve canonical identity while allowing pose and action to change.

Keep multi-panel storyboards, identity references, environment references, and single first/end frames separate. Never feed the whole sheet as one first frame. Select the H3 mode from the actual task and supported attachment roles. First/end frame pairs must be reachable within the time budget. A continuity handoff carries velocity and action phase as well as pose; a repeated frame is not an instruction to pause. Respect hard cuts and actual predecessor-reference support.

Compile the approved plan using the current H3 representation accepted by the adapter. Do not mix client briefing templates with final model syntax or invent camera API fields. Keep exact speech in supported audio/dialogue locations, action in picture prose, and music separate from in-world sound. Match reference labels to actual attached assets. Describe a few concrete, ordered actions instead of a list of cinematic adjectives and contradictory negative prompts.

## Worked beat (direction notes, not importer JSON)

A six-second silent reveal: in a medium two-shot, she places an envelope on the table during the opening beat; he reaches, opens it and exposes a photograph; the remaining time belongs to recognition and her choice to step away. Keep the camera fixed or make a short motivated dolly toward the photograph with a clear path. Board a representative reveal instant and describe the preceding placement and following reaction in prose. Do not require a hand or envelope to hover for the shot duration. If the handling cannot read naturally in six seconds, simplify the handling or lengthen/split coverage within supported segment limits.

## Review

Check source fidelity, motivated coverage, natural delivery, event timing, support/contact chains, credible camera paths, spatial continuity and board-to-motion roles before serialization. Then validate schema, archive layout, references and H3 syntax separately. A valid package and attractive stills cannot establish motion quality. Review rendered video and audio only when generation is authorized; report untested motion honestly. Skill editing authorizes no image generation, video generation or live movie import.
