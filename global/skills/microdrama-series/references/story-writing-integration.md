# Story-writing integration for direct microdrama

Use the installed `story-init`, `character-management`, `plot-structure`, `chapter-writing`, `scene-craft` and `revision-continuity` skills for their respective stages. Read their SKILL.md and relevant references before applying them. Use `better-writing` when available for prose quality. Discover skills from the current catalog rather than hardcoding machine paths. If a named skill is unavailable, disclose that and use the bundled adaptation below; do not claim to have loaded it or require an unrelated installation. This adaptation keeps the packaged workflow usable without local dependencies.

## Adaptation and authority

Story skills own story development. Director v9 owns film duration, dialogue occupancy, camera/shot plan, H3 compilation and importer fields. An episode draft is a short dramatic story unit: chapter-writing's outline-first method does not impose novel chapter length or prose word counts on screen runtime. Preserve source language, cultural setting, locked lines and canon. Separate user-supplied facts from newly developed material. For an existing locked story, seek clarification before a consequential canon change; for a summary that explicitly invites development, create the missing story details within that brief.

1. **Initialize (story-init).** Record premise, genre, audience, language, style, central question and intended ending in `story.md`. Maintain characters, plot, scenes, chapters and continuity indexes using the installed skill's templates when available. Existing project structure takes precedence over duplicate scaffolding.
2. **Characters (character-management).** Establish wants, needs, opposing motives, relationships, voice patterns and change across the season. Define stable identifiers and mutual relationship links. Story voice is distinct from an audio voice-sample binding.
3. **Season and episode outline (plot-structure).** Choose a structure that fits the genre. Plan setup, escalation, climax and resolution before polishing the opening. Episode grid columns: ID, source mapping, target runtime, opening hook, objective, obstruction, turn, ending hook, promised next payoff. Preserve explicit episode divisions; develop summary-only boundaries by causality. Maintain reveal and promise records with planted/payoff episode and status.
4. **Draft (chapter-writing + scene-craft).** Gather bible, character/location context, previous end state and active arcs. Outline beats before prose; apply any user-requested outline review once rather than repeatedly seeking the same approval. Each scene records viewpoint, participants, objective, conflict, meaningful change, location and state changes. Use reaction/dilemma/decision when the consequence calls for it, purposeful try/fail escalation, distinct dialogue voices and subtext. Draft complete episodes directly into Markdown. Avoid filler, repeated exposition, unearned twists and cliffhangers created by discontinuity. Convert internal thoughts into observable action or motivated narration during screenplay adaptation.
5. **Revise (revision-continuity).** Review the entire season, not just its opening: causality, knowledge, time/travel, prop ownership, emotional progression, reveals and payoff. Compare actual drafts against the outline. Preserve a recoverable draft before substantial revision without making unauthorized Git commits. Update scene state, timeline, arcs and promises after edits; perform registry/link checks manually if the Story CLI is unavailable.
6. **Adapt to v9.** Write the season screenplay and each episode's `movie.movie` through the available MovieScript DSL procedure. Preserve line ownership and map scene beats to timed segments. Calculate runtime from authored shot windows and plausible delivery, including pauses, reactions and action. Split or edit within authorized scope instead of squeezing dialogue into an impossible duration. Keep the full story draft available for traceability.

## Authoring evidence

Keep an episode runtime grid, setup/payoff ledger, character knowledge and physical state ledger, canonical asset registry, saved-voice mapping and episode boundary handoffs. These may be Markdown/JSON supporting documents but must not introduce unsupported importer fields. Record the final source version used by the Director pass. If story edits change assets, dialogue, physical states or shot count, invalidate and rebuild affected prompts/boards before packaging.

Default only when unspecified: six episodes, 60–90 seconds each, 9:16. These are adjustable user-workflow defaults, not universal microdrama rules. An episode may contain multiple scenes, and a scene may continue across episode boundaries. End each episode on a consequential hook; resolve the main season question in the finale while leaving the user's requested forward hook.
